Ada Lovelace

Software Engineer

Ada Lovelace was an English mathematician and writer best known for her work on Charles Babbage’s early mechanical computer, the Analytical Engine. She was born in 1815, the daughter of the poet Lord Byron, but was raised primarily by her mother, who encouraged her education in mathematics and science. In 1843, Lovelace translated an article about the Analytical Engine and added her own extensive notes, which were longer than the original text. In these notes, she described how the machine could be programmed to calculate Bernoulli numbers, making her the first person to publish what is considered the first computer algorithm. She also foresaw that computers could do more than calculations, suggesting they might create music or graphics. Because of this visionary insight, she is often regarded as the world’s first computer programmer. Today, she is celebrated as a pioneer in computer science, and the programming language “Ada” is named in her honor.
